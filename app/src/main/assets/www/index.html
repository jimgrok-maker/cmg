Water

Check official NOAA / CHS chart for depths. CMG stays inside a coarse shoreline. Planning aid — not a chartplotter. Island routing is detailed on Erie first.

What is CMG?

A trip planner with a live SOG and gold GPS track. Not a chartplotter. Always check NOAA / CHS for depths.

Leave-at

Default is now. Set a later departure and every gold tag and polar hour shifts to that clock. Use it for an overnight so dusk and dawn get the right HRRR hours.

TWD FROM vs the arrow

Numbers are meteorological FROM. Gold barbs point downwind. Gxx on a barb is gust knots at that hour.

Route colors

Red is no-go, under 40°. Orange is pinched (40–55°). Yellow is tight (55–80°). Green is a reach (80–140°). Slate is deep (140–165°). Brown is a dead run (over 165°). Same yellow is no longer used for a run.

Where does the wind come from?

Open-Meteo HRRR (~3 km cells, hourly). After the first route, CMG samples up to 12 cells along the track (~every 8 nm) and times each cell to when you are predicted to be there.

No cell signal

The last center forecast and the last 12-cell field are saved on the phone. Offline, CMG keeps using them and marks the age. A refresh runs every 60 minutes when a fetch works.

P50 / P90

P50 is polar time in the forecast we have. P90 is that time × 1.22 — lulls, slop, and a forecast that was 20° off. Not a second model. A sail-only no-go leg is drawn dashed and left out of the P50. The clock is the solid line only.

SOG vs polar

After about 20 minutes of GPS motion, CMG compares live SOG to the planned polar average.

Motor policy

Default is sail only. Hybrid compare motors at 4 kn when VMG is under 2 kn.

Fence vs chart

The thin line around a lake and dashed island hulls are a wet/dry test, not the shoreline. OSM under the map is the shore. GPS track ignores the fence.

GPX

Export GPX shares the planned route (marks plus any auto detour) to Files or OpenCPN. Planning stays in CMG; the last mile stays on a real chart.

Islands

Erie has keep-off hulls for Pelee, Middle Island, Bass, Kelleys. Ohio inland lakes use a coarse reservoir outline only — add taps around coves.
